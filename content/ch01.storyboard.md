# Chapter 1 figure storyboard: `order-line-journey`

Companion to `content/ch01.ts` (figure key `order-line-journey`, `figureStep` 0 to 7). Rules come from `DESIGN.md` (sections 1.2, 1.3, 1.6, 1.7, 2.1) and are not repeated here, only applied. Colours are tokens (`--ink`, `--ink-2`, `--pencil`, `--accent`, `--paper-2`). No hex in code.

## 0. What the figure is

One continuous drawing on a sheet of about 620 x 520 (coordinates below are in that space, origin top-left of the drawing box, inside the page padding). Three columns, read left to right: **source** (NetSuite records), **warehouse** (tables), **semantic** (business names and the report). One persistent thread, the **order-line thread** `T`, starts at line 1 of the form and is extended at every step until it reaches the report cell. Earlier segments stay drawn; each segment keeps the line style of its own provenance (so `T` is a mix of pencil, solid and stitched). Only the segment and element that the current step is about take the accent.

State is a pure function of `figureStep`: step N shows everything introduced in steps 0 to N. Nothing is ever removed or faded out. Scrolling back to step N-1 re-renders that state (elements introduced later are simply not present).

### Shapes (DESIGN 1.6)

- Source: plain rectangle, 0.8 px outline, 0 degree hatch band, name in mono in **source case** (`transactionline`, `Transactions`, `customer`), 10 px so `transactionaccountingline` fits 150 px.
- Warehouse: punched tag, 1.6 px outline, 45 degree hatch band, mono UPPERCASE 11 px. Only the focal tag of a step may carry the 1.6 weight at full strength; others still follow 1.6 (it is the layer rule) and the accent marks focus.
- Semantic: pill, 1.1 px outline, 135 degree hatch, serif 12 px.
- Derived: double hexagon (two 0.6 outlines 3 px apart), stipple, serif italic.
- Row strips and the form body: hairline 0.6 rectangles on `--paper-2`, mono 10.5 px text. These are data, not layers.
- The form is an invented object: drawn as a source-shaped rectangle with a **pencil** outline.

### Line styles (DESIGN 1.7), and the one rule used throughout

- **Solid ink, 1.1 px**: the data states it (a source field lands in a warehouse column; a column serves a presentation column).
- **Stitched thread**: inferred. Used for every fact-to-dimension link (the data shows co-occurrence in subject areas, never a join), for the `_SEC` twin (named, not proven) and for the item, day and accounting-book links.
- **Pencil**: invented, or stated by Oracle's documentation and absent from the lineage file (the form, the accounting-line record, `SOURCE_RECORD_ID`, the second accounting book, the report).
- A legend (DESIGN section 5) is drawn from step 1 on, in the lower left, x 16 to 176, y 440 to 492 (three samples). It is the one place that uses y > 428 in the source column.

### Accent

Exactly one accent group per step (one focus id). Every other element is ink or pencil. Hover or keyboard focus on any tag moves the accent to that tag and its thread and drops everything else to 30% for the duration (160 ms), then returns to the step's own focus.

### Draw order inside a transition

Frame/outline first, then threads (stitched ones sewn one whole stitch at a time with `steps(n)`), then the tag each thread reaches, then hatch band, then labels (written in by clip), then row values (opacity 320 ms), then callout letters and leaders, last. Elements already present do not redraw. Transition length 1.8 s at most, stagger 120 ms between groups.

## 2. Phone width (below 560 px)

The figure rebuilds, not scales. One column, width = container. Order top to bottom: form, five records (each 36 tall), warehouse tags with their strips, pills, report. Threads become vertical elbows in a 20 px channel on the left edge (x 0 to 20); channel B stitched links run on the right edge. Tags keep 11 px text down to 280 px; callouts drop their phrase and keep the letter when they would touch a tag. Height is auto (about 1100 px at 340 px wide); the figure stays in the step's scroll area and the step's changes remain the same adds-per-step, so on a phone it is a tall drawing that fills in down the page. If the owner prefers a pinned figure on phones, that is an open question.

## 4. Things the figure must never state

Data types, join conditions, formulas, refresh timing, which columns are measures, and the real format of `source_record_id`. Where a step needs one of these to teach, it is drawn in pencil and labelled "invented" or "not in the data" as above.
